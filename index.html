The work, in depth

Three projects, four questions each: what was hard, what was chosen, what it costs and what proved it.

Decision Architecture

The four-book thesis and the two instruments that make it executable: Fulcrum and LatencyLab.

The hard problem

Organisational theory cannot be run: claims arrive in prose and reorgs get tested on real people.

The decision

Make it executable. Fulcrum scores structural health 0 to 100 with no randomness: same model, same number, every time.

The trade-off accepted

The units are abstract, not calibrated to your KPIs. And the model never says what to decide, only where decisions can live.

The evidence

Typical archetypes collapse 75.6 to 14.8 as they grow; well-designed ones hold 98.7 to 66.4. Falsifiable by design.

Protocol & standards

The MMSP specification and Meridian, its reference implementation.

The hard problem

Publishers now ship video, audio, articles and releases; RSS models one form well and push alternatives monetise attention.

The decision

A protocol, not a platform: JSON, pull-only, a semantic superset of RSS 2.0 and Atom, calm enforced at the wire level.

The trade-off accepted

No push and a 300-second poll floor: real-time delivery is impossible, on purpose. A community spec, not an IETF work product.

The evidence

A conformance suite covers every normative statement; Meridian proves the rules against real feeds with 100% branch coverage.

Applications

Shipped applications, led by PigeonPost (email, calendar, contacts) and ClearBudget (solvency forecasting).

The hard problem

Webmail wants engagement, Outlook wants a subscription and Thunderbird's priorities sit in someone else's queue; in budgeting, a monthly total can look safe while the month itself is structurally unsafe.

The decision

Local-first, structurally: PigeonPost keeps mail with your provider (no account, no relay, no telemetry); ClearBudget projects the month day by day instead of reporting where the money went.

The trade-off accepted

The local cache is not encrypted at rest; the site says so and points to OS disk encryption. Openly not for webmail-only users.

The evidence

Installers for Windows, macOS and Linux behind a 100% coverage gate on the core logic. Both are the author's own daily tools.
